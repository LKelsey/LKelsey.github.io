# My Research
{: .page-title}

## Type Ia Supernovae

Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) result from the thermonuclear explosion of a white dwarf star, which releases energy through the radioactive decay of Nickel-56. This provides a constraint on possible brightnesses of the explosion, enabling the use of SNe Ia as "standardisable candles" for measuring distances in the universe and uncovering the accelerating expansion of the universe.

### Host Galaxy Properties

SNe Ia in high-mass galaxies are brighter than SNe Ia in low-mass galaxies. This suggests additional physics at play, such as unknown progenitor systems or explosion mechanisms. Taking the properties of the host galaxies, and the local environments of the SNe, into account in cosmological analyses is the focus of my research, with highlights in [Kelsey et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.501.4861K/abstract) and [Kelsey et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.519.3046K/abstract).

### Supernova Siblings

SNe that share the same host galaxy ("siblings") share global environmental properties and associated systematic uncertainties. By comparing siblings, we can determine if sub-galactic environmental dependencies are the dominant factor in SNe Ia cosmology.

I've built the largest sample of SNe Ia siblings to date ([Kelsey 2024](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230302020K/abstract)), and currently lead a [Hubble Space Telescope program (#17194)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022hst..prop17194K/abstract) studying their local environments.
